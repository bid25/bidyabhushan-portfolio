The problem

The number on your screen is fiction.

Food diaries checked against doubly-labelled water land on one finding: people under-report what they eat by 10 to 20 percent, and they do it honestly. It is a measurement problem wearing a discipline problem's clothes.

The tolerance bar

Every number carries its error.

Portion error and database error are independent, so they add in quadrature. Summing them would make the app look useless. Dropping one would make it a liar. A weighed, labelled food still carries ±10.2%, and Broccoli says so. Move the sliders.

Illustration: entries of about 150 kcal each. Weighed ±10%, barcode ±18%, AI photo ±28%, typed ±45%.

Where the ± comes from

Portion is how it was measured: a scale is tight, an eyeball is loose. Database is how good the food record is: lab data is tight, crowdsourced is loose. The band is the long side, and it is what you see on the bar.

band = √(portion² + database²)

Questions

Straight answers.

Do I need an account?

No. Guest mode is complete. An account adds photo scans, backup and sync, and signing up keeps everything you already logged.

Does it really work with no signal?

Search, logging, barcodes already seen, recipes, charts and the coach all run on the phone. Only AI photo scans, new-product lookups, sign-in and sync need a connection.

Where does my data go?

Signed out, nothing leaves the phone except a barcode string for a new product. No analytics, no ad IDs, and health data is scrubbed from crash reports. Export everything, or delete your account, for free.

Is it medical advice?

No. Broccoli is an instrument, not a medical device. Talk to a healthcare professional before big changes to your diet.
